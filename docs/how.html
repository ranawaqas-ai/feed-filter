Feed Filter

How it works

What it does

You write your goals in plain words. On YouTube's home feed and sidebar, every video starts blurred. The extension sends its title, channel, length and view count to Jev, a TypeSafe model that answers one question: does this video serve this goal?

Clear matches get an On goal badge. Clear misses are removed. Anything in between is shown as usual.

You stay in control

You can reveal what was filtered and see why. A video in the grey area asks "On goal?" on its thumbnail, with Yes and No. An on-goal video can be kept or hidden from its three-dot menu. Shorts, playlists and ads can be removed without scoring, and so can whole channels or languages.

When most of what YouTube offers is rejected, a notice above the feed says so and gives you a search for each goal. What you watch next shapes what YouTube recommends after.

A dashboard shows what got through, by goal and over time.

Install

  1. Download feed-filter-<version>.zip from the latest release and unzip it into a folder you will keep. Chrome loads the extension from there.
  2. Open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, choose Load unpacked and pick the unzipped folder (the one with manifest.json).
  3. In the setup page that opens, answer two questions to make your first goal, paste a key from console.typesafe.ai and set a daily limit. It then opens YouTube and shows how many videos on your home feed serve the goal.

Until setup is saved, YouTube looks as usual. Desktop Chrome only. To update, unzip the new release over the same folder and click reload on the extension's card.

What it costs

The extension is free. Scoring uses your own TypeSafe key: one call per video per goal, with scores cached for 30 days. A long evening with three goals can use a few thousand calls. You set a daily limit, and past it new videos stay blurred until the next day.

What leaves your browser

Each video's ID, title, channel, length and view count, with your goal text, sent to TypeSafe. No cookies or account details. Your key, scores and history stay in your Chrome profile.

Optional: if you add an Anthropic key, the titles and channel names of videos YouTube offered you and videos you watched are sent to Claude once a week, to show your feed by topic on the dashboard. It is off until you enter that key.
